Community Sizing Hypothesis

Our growth model prioritizes social cohesion over raw numbers, using a networked approach to balance intimacy with diversity.

Phase 1: The Pioneer Group (30-50 Members)

The smallest viable community size. Large enough for a diverse skill set to establish core food and energy systems, yet small enough for deep trust and rapid, informal decision-making. The primary challenge at this stage is mitigating groupthink; hence, the Values-Alignment Questionnaire is critical for selecting pioneers committed to radical acceptance.

Phase 2: The Dunbar Unit (100-150 Members)

The optimal size for a single, stable community, based on Dunbar's Number. This size supports a rich social fabric, preventing the claustrophobia of smaller groups while maintaining a high-trust environment where everyone is known. Formal systems like the DAO and conflict resolution frameworks become essential to manage increased social complexity.

Phase 3: The Networked Civilization (Scaling Out, Not Up)

To achieve greater diversity and scale, we will not grow a single community beyond the Dunbar limit. Instead, we will replicate successful communities, creating a "fractal" network of interconnected 150-person units. These independent communities are united by the global Luminary Nexus DAO, the Passport system, and a shared set of core values, offering the best of both small-town intimacy and global-network diversity.

The Utopia Passport & Travel Coin System

A system designed to make inter-community travel an effortless, equitable, and celebrated right of membership, fostering a truly interconnected network.

Travel Coins: A Non-Financial Currency

Members receive an annual, non-transferable allotment of Travel Coins. These are used to "pay" for stays in other communities. The cost is dynamic, based on demand, ensuring fair access to all locations. This prevents desirable "destination" communities from being monopolized.

The "Living Bounty Contract": Flexible Rewards

Members completing bounties can choose their reward (LNX or Travel Coins). They can change their preference at any time before the bounty is complete, allowing for flexibility as personal needs change during long-term projects.

The "Sponsored Journey": Compassionate Gifting

Members can use their own Travel Coins to gift a specific trip to another member. This creates a non-transferable "Gifted Journey Ticket," allowing for acts of kindness without creating a secondary market. To prevent social friction, if the recipient declines the gift, cannot take the trip, or passes away, the Travel Coins are automatically and fully refunded to the giver.

The "Active Explorer" Rule & Coin Maturity

Travel Coins mature after 18 months. Members who have actively traveled (met a DAO-set threshold) within that period are given a 24-hour choice to book a final trip or convert their maturing coins to LNX. If a member has not traveled, they are only offered the 24-hour window to book a trip. Unused coins from non-travelers are returned to a "Community Travel Pool" to fund travel stipends for all, ensuring contributions always benefit the network.

Passport Stamps (SBTs): Celebrating Exploration

Upon visiting a community, members can claim a unique, non-transferable digital badge (Soulbound Token) for their Nexus Hub profile. This gamifies travel, encourages exploration, and creates a visual record of a member's engagement with the wider network.

The Nexus Transport Enterprise

A DAO-owned fleet of autonomous electric vehicles (EVs) that serves as both an internal utility and an economic and social bridge to the outside world.

Dual-Purpose Fleet

The fleet provides seamless, integrated transport between communities for members using Travel Coins. Simultaneously, it operates as a public ride-share service for surrounding towns, generating income for the DAO treasury.

The "Community Lift" Program

A portion of the fleet's operational time is dedicated to providing free, essential transport for needy individuals in surrounding communities, managed through partnerships with local social service organizations. This is a core outreach initiative.

Open-Source & Fair Bounties

The autonomous driving system will be based on open-source platforms like Openpilot, providing a development project for community members. Maintenance and cleaning are handled via the Nexus Hub bounty board, with reward values pragmatically scaled to the difficulty of the task (e.g., higher LNX for sanitizing a medical transport vehicle).

Marketplace Framework

A three-tiered economic system designed to ensure internal needs are met as a basic right, while fostering a healthy economic relationship with the wider world.

Tier 1: Internal Member Access

For essential goods produced by the community (e.g., food from agrarian pods), members pay a nominal, fixed price in LNX (e.g., 1 LNX). This establishes that basic needs are a right of membership, not a commercial transaction.

Tier 2: External (LNX Discount)

The public-facing Gateway Marketplace offers goods and services to non-members. A discounted price is offered to those who pay in LNX, creating a gentle incentive for external partners to adopt and use our native token.

Tier 3: External (Standard)

The standard market price for non-members paying in traditional currency or stablecoins. Revenue from these sales directly funds the DAO treasury, contributing to the community's long-term financial sustainability.

Advanced DAO Governance

Evolved governance mechanisms designed to promote fairness, prevent centralization, and distribute cultural energy across the entire network.

The "Touring Festival" Model

To prevent any single community from becoming a cultural "capital," major events like festivals will be designed to tour the network. The host locations will be determined by the DAO, ensuring that all communities have the opportunity to host and benefit from these high-energy gatherings.

Regional & Weighted Voting

For selecting event locations, the DAO will use a two-phase process. First, members vote for a general *region* (e.g., Utopia-North-America-SW). The region with the most collective votes wins. Second, the final host community within that region is chosen by a weighted lottery, giving smaller communities a fair chance to be selected. This prevents the largest communities from always winning and fosters inter-regional collaboration.
